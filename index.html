// ABOUT

I'm an embedded systems engineer at Techmology, where for the past 3+ years I've taken hardware products from concept to production — schematic capture, PCB layout, component selection, firmware, board bring-up, and the lab-bench debugging that turns a prototype into something that actually ships. I also lead product and development there, overseeing execution and client-facing outcomes on top of the hands-on engineering work.

Day to day that's meant designing educational robotics and IoT hardware end to end, and writing firmware for STM32 and ESP32 based boards — motor control, sensor acquisition, and communication over UART, I²C, SPI, and CAN. Alongside that, I'm a doctoral researcher at the University of Blida 1, in partnership with CDTA, contributing to QoSoC — a low-power RISC-V SoC fabricated on IHP's open 130nm PDK — more of a technical edge than the headline.

08

QoSoC — Low-Power RISC-V SoC

A taped-out system-on-chip for soil quality monitoring, built on an open 130nm PDK.

QoSoC is a low-power RISC-V SoC designed for soil quality monitoring in IoT deployments, built in collaboration with a team at CDTA. It was my first full IC design from architecture through to tapeout, targeting IHP's open SG13G2 130nm process. The project covered the whole stack: core integration, peripheral design, low-power strategy, and the verification work needed to sign off a real chip rather than just an FPGA prototype.
